Abusive or misleading public playlists can now be moderated from the operator panel in two tiers. The API gains `GET /admin/playlists` (public playlists only, search by title, `status=active|deactivated|all` defaulting to active, `ownerId`, and a `createdAt`/`title` sort allowlist), `GET /admin/playlists/:id` with the owner and the first 50 tracks in playlist order, `PATCH /admin/playlists/:id/visibility` with `{ isPublic, reason? }`, `DELETE /admin/playlists/:id` (soft delete) and `POST /admin/playlists/:id/restore`, behind the new `playlists:read`, `playlists:hide`, `playlists:delete` and `playlists:restore` permissions. Hiding is the gentler tier: it forces the playlist private without deleting it. Taking it down is the harder tier: it soft-deletes it. The two are independent, so restore never re-publishes a hidden playlist and un-hiding never restores a taken-down one. An un-hide is only accepted when the latest operator visibility action on the playlist was a hide, so an operator can never publish a playlist its owner made private. The panel adds `/playlists` and `/playlists/:id` with confirm dialogs and an optional reason, a Playlists entry in the sidebar, and a link from a moderation report's playlist subject to its detail page. Only `playlists:read` and `playlists:hide` join the built-in MODERATOR template, and that template only seeds the role on first boot: existing moderators gain nothing until an administrator edits them on `staff/:id`; `playlists:delete` and `playlists:restore` stay administrator-only. Because no column records who hid a playlist, a hidden playlist leaves the list (which shows public playlists only) and is reached through a report, the audit log or its URL rather than the list.
